## A1. Where is the legal line for "in the style of X" games? (copyright idea/expression, trade dress, trademark, enforcement history, Palworld, clone cases)

### Takeaway
US law protects a game's *expression* (specific audiovisual look, arbitrary design choices, characters, art, music, level layouts, distinctive "look and feel") but not its *ideas* (genre, rules, mechanics, loop). "Make a farming-sim with a day cycle, relationships and pixel art" is on the safe side of the line; replicating Stardew Valley's sprites, portraits, map, names, UI chrome or logo is not. Trademark/trade dress adds a separate risk whenever a named game is used in marketing or in a way that implies endorsement. Nintendo's Palworld suit is a *patent* case (not copyright) and has largely collapsed; Nintendo and Take-Two nonetheless have an aggressive DMCA record against fan content on UGC platforms.

### Cited Findings
**Copyright: idea vs. expression**
- Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive (D.N.J., May 30, 2012, 863 F.Supp.2d 394): Xio argued it copied only rules/functional features; the court held there is no copyright in game mechanics or rules (ideas) but Tetris Holding was entitled to protection for "the way in which it chose to express these game mechanics in the form of the audio-visual features of the game." Summary judgment granted to Tetris on copyright **and trade dress** — [Proskauer New Media Law](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2012/06/19/videogame-app-developer-breaks-the-rules-on-copyright-infringement/); [Fed Bar IP Insight PDF](https://www.fedbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/ipinsight-octnov2012-pdf-1.pdf); [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/?p=411)
- The Tetris court rejected the "functionality" defense: an expressive feature is not limited to one implementation, and stays protected unless so inseparable from the idea that there are few other ways to express it; Xio had copied *arbitrary* choices not required by the rules (specific colors, board dimensions, piece shapes/motion) — [Lexplug outline](https://www.lexplug.com/outlines/entertainment-law/video-games-interactive-media); [Stones Law summary](https://www.stoneslaw.net/cloning-video-games-is-copyright-infringement) ("Taking a single one of these elements may not have been infringement, but wholesale replication was.")
- Commentators note the holding is considered expansive/controversial and that for simple games the idea/expression line is "often blurred" — [Jesper Juul](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/page/28/); [Mallesons Pulse](https://pulse.mallesons.com/?p=682)
- Spry Fox v. LOLApps (Triple Town vs Yeti Town; W.D. Wash., Judge Richard A. Jones, Sept/Oct 2012 — sources differ on the date): motion to dismiss denied; court found Spry Fox "plausibly alleged substantial similarity," reasoning that game mechanics get little protection while the clone's style/expression (progression of objects, visual/narrative elements) was still actionable; "look and feel" treated as potentially copyrightable, following Tetris. Case settled Oct 2012 with Yeti Town's IP transferred to Spry Fox — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spry_Fox,_LLC_v._Lolapps,_Inc.); [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=91469); [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=10653); [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/?p=5131); [MCV settlement](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/triple-town-cloning-suit-settled/)
- Copyright infringement requires showing access + substantial similarity of protected elements; trade dress rights, unlike copyright, arise only from *use* and source identification, not from creation — [Stones Law](https://www.stoneslaw.net/cloning-video-games-is-copyright-infringement)

**Trade dress / trademark**
- Tetris won on trade dress as well as copyright (the look of the game served as a source identifier) — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2012/06/19/videogame-app-developer-breaks-the-rules-on-copyright-infringement/)
- Recent trade-dress/trademark clone matters (2025): Ubisoft v. Well Go USA alleges the game "Assassins" infringes the trademark and trade dress of Assassin's Creed; Aristocrat's "Dragon Link" slot trade-dress claims were allowed to proceed as potentially source-identifying; Homa Games v. Century Games (C.D. Cal., filed Jan 2025) alleges "All in Hole" was copied "level by level and element by element" under copyright — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/video-games-13/); [Lexis Legal News](https://www.lexislegalnews.com/mealeys/amp/articles/1661626); [Esports Legal News](https://esportslegal.news/?p=4127)
- Nintendo v. Genki (C.D. Cal., filed May 2, 2025): claims of trademark infringement, unfair competition and false advertising over Switch 2 mockups shown at CES 2025; Nintendo sought to bar use of the "Switch" name and treble damages; reportedly settled with damages and a permanent injunction against using Nintendo logos/designs or anything confusing — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nintendo-is-suing-the-accessory-manufacturer-that-showed-off-switch-2-early); [TechSpot settlement thread](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/nintendo-settles-lawsuit-with-genki-over-pre-release-switch-2-mockups-at-ces-2025.294645/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/nintendo-switch-2-mockup-lawsuit-3554817). Relevance: naming a franchise in *marketing* (not just inside a prompt) is the trademark exposure.

**Palworld / Nintendo (patent, not copyright)**
- Nintendo + The Pokémon Company sued Pocketpair in Tokyo District Court (Sept 2024) for **patent** infringement (JP patents 7545191, 7493117, 7528390 — creature-capture via thrown item, capture-rate calculation, riding), seeking an injunction and 5M yen each; explicitly not a copyright/character-design claim — [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/nintendo-and-the-pokemon-company-seek-an-injuction-and-damages-from-palworld-developer-pocketpair); [GoNintendo legal analysis](https://gonintendo.com/comments/14232)
- Status as of mid/late 2026: Pocketpair patched out the disputed mechanics; US and JP patent offices pushed back on related patents; plaintiffs narrowed claims to older pre-patch versions sold in Japan; no injunction granted; Palworld 1.0 launched July 10, 2026; technical hearing Oct 1, 2026 and preliminary disclosure of court's views set for Nov 9, 2026; analysts estimate recoverable damages ~US$30K — [GamesFray](https://gamesfray.com/ahead-of-october-1-court-hearing-nintendo-has-zero-chance-of-prevailing-over-current-palworld-versions-it-may-get-30k-chump-change/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/nintendo-wanted-to-block-palworld-now-it-faces-a-0-percent-chance-and-a-measly-usd30k-payout); [Lawfold tracker (Aug 2026)](https://lawfold.com/palworld-nintendo-pokemon-lawsuit/). No reporting found on the Oct 1 hearing outcome.

**Enforcement history against UGC/fan content**
- Nintendo: takedown notice to Game Jolt (2016) over fan games; 60+ DMCA notices in June 2024 against a mod-hosting site (Tears of the Kingdom, Smash Ultimate mods); Mario content purged from Garry's Mod (Facepunch said "the takedowns came from Nintendo"; later reports suggest some notices may have been from an impersonator — unverified) — [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/nintendo-targets-fan-games-in-dmca-takedown-12127); [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/36616-nintendo-sends-out-wave-of-dmca-takedowns-for-game-mods); [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/garrys-mod/)
- Nintendo v. Yuzu (Tropic Haze) settled March 2024: $2.4M, emulator and Citra shut down, domain surrendered — [SI Videogames](https://videogames.si.com/news/nintendo-switch-emulator-yuzu-lawsuit-settled); [Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/switch-emulator-yuzu-shuts-down-after-settling-nintendo-lawsuit-for-2-4-million-883489)
- Dreams (Media Molecule/Sony, PS4): in March 2020 Sony's legal team removed a Super Mario creation at Nintendo's request; Sony said removals are handled case by case; Media Molecule has a policy for removing infringing content; the creator could no longer edit the original — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2020/03/sony_intends_to_remove_more_nintendo_projects_from_its_ps4_exclusive_dreams); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/dreams-super-mario-removed/)
- Take-Two: CEO said the company is "pretty flexible" on GTA mods but sends notices when "the economy is threatened, or if there's bad behaviour"; July 2021 wave hit decade-old mods; DMCA to a VR mod creator for GTA V / RDR2 / Mafia demanding removal from Patreon; criteria never clarified — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/take-two-claims-it-is-pretty-flexible-on-removing-gta-mods-3009849); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/news/take-two-dmca-gta-red-dead-mafia-vr-mods)

### Inferences
- For an AI builder, the dangerous outputs are the ones that reproduce *expression*: named characters, recognizable sprite/portrait art, specific maps/level layouts, UI chrome, logos, music, dialogue, and the exact "arbitrary" visual choices (Tetris's piece colors/shapes; Triple Town's object progression and bear characters). Genre, loop, camera, perspective, mechanics, rule systems and general art *style* (pixel art, hand-painted, 16-bit palette) are ideas or unprotectable style.
- The Palworld case shows the next frontier is *patents on mechanics* (JP-centric); a web 2D platform is unlikely to be targeted, but it is a reason not to literally clone a specific patented mechanic implementation when the reference game is from a litigious publisher (Nintendo, Take-Two, Warner's nemesis system).
- Trademark exposure arises mainly from *marketing and discoverability* ("Stardew-like" in a title, tag, store page or ad), and from confusion; keeping named games out of public-facing titles/metadata (while allowing them in the private prompt) is the lowest-cost mitigation.
- Nintendo and Take-Two treat UGC platforms as enforcement targets; a platform hosting user games needs a DMCA agent, repeat-infringer policy and quick takedown + strike workflow (see A3/A4).

## A2. How do current AI image/game tools handle a named franchise in a prompt (allow, soft-block, rewrite)?

### Takeaway
As of 2026 the big vendors have moved from "allow by default + DMCA" (2024–early 2025) toward hard blocks on specific rights-holders' characters (Gemini blocks Disney after a Dec 2025 cease-and-desist; OpenAI moved Sora 2 to opt-in for characters in Oct 2025) while still allowing *style* descriptions. Midjourney relies on ToS prohibitions plus DMCA, and is being sued by Disney/Universal/Warner over character outputs. No game-generation vendor (Rosebud, Genie) publishes a named-game prompt policy.

### Cited Findings
- Disney and Universal sued Midjourney (C.D. Cal., June 2025) alleging it generates their characters (Darth Vader, Elsa, Minions, Homer Simpson); Warner Bros. Discovery filed separately Sept 2025 and the cases were combined in Nov 2025; court suggested mediation (Nov 2025); discovery dispute hearing set May 22, 2026; no settlement or trial date found — [LA Mag](https://lamag.com/news/disney-and-universal-sue-ai-company-midjourney-for-copyright-infringement/); [Graphic Policy, Nov 2025](https://graphicpolicy.com/2025/11/14/court-suggests-disney-v-midjourney-go-to-mediation/); [Graphic Policy, May 2026](https://graphicpolicy.com/2026/05/11/disney-warner-bros-universal-and-midjourney-discovery-dispute/)
- Midjourney ToS: users may not use the service "to try to violate the intellectual property rights of others, including copyright, patent, or trademark rights"; for Editor/Video users warrant they will not "enter prompts ... in any way that infringes third-party intellectual property"; DMCA takedowns via takedowns@midjourney.com; repeat infringers terminated; Midjourney states it "cannot offer guidance on copyright matters" — [Midjourney ToS](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32083055291277-Terms-of-Service); [Community Guidelines](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32013696484109-Community-Guidelines)
- OpenAI Sora 2 (launched ~Sept 30, 2025): initially required rights-holders to *opt out* and reportedly refused Disney/Marvel but widely generated other US and Japanese game/anime characters; within ~3 days OpenAI reversed to an **opt-in** model with "more granular control" for rights-holders and floated revenue sharing — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-sora-2-copyright-opt-out/); [Copyright Lately](https://copyrightlately.com/openai-backtracks-sora-opt-out-copyright-policy/); [Yahoo/WSJ](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/openais-sora-video-generator-require-194118045.html). The ChatGPT image launch (April 2025) was opt-out-style and produced the "Ghibli-style" wave — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/openais-sora-bet-copyright-first-permission-later.md)
- Google Gemini: in March 2025 Gemini 2.0 Flash "will uncomplainingly create images depicting celebrities and copyrighted characters" (TechCrunch test); Disney sent a cease-and-desist in Dec 2025; by early 2026 Gemini denies generating "any Disney-related characters and imagery" while still generating non-Disney characters such as Shrek — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2981172); [Android Authority, Disney C&D](https://www.androidauthority.com/disney-google-ai-cease-and-desist-3624592); [Android Authority, Gemini blocks Disney](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-gemini-blocks-disney-generation-3639914); [Gemini policy guidelines](https://gemini.google/safety-goals)
- Google DeepMind's Genie prompt guide frames prompts as environment + controllable character + world sketch, with no named-IP guidance; practitioners report "style anchors" (palette, camera angle, texture cues) and 1–2 mechanical primitives (move, jump, collide, collect) work best — [DeepMind Genie prompt guide](https://deepmind.google/models/genie/prompt-guide/); [Macaron review](https://macaron.im/blog/genie-3-capabilities-limitations)
- Rosebud AI: no published named-game prompt policy found; ToS requires users to hold rights to uploads and disclaims liability for user content — [Rosebud ToS](https://rosebud.ai/terms-of-service) (search-index excerpt; page ~15 months old per index)

### Inferences
- The industry direction is: *style/genre words allowed; specific characters/marks hard-blocked for litigious rights-holders; opt-in licensing for everything else*. A platform launching in 2026 should assume that outputs depicting named characters are the thing that triggers suits and C&Ds, and that "prompt rewriting to attributes" (what Gemini and OpenAI effectively do when they refuse the character but honor the aesthetic) is the de-facto norm.
- None of the vendors *show the user what was extracted* from a franchise reference; that transparency is a differentiator rather than a copy of existing practice (see A3).

### Gaps
- No primary OpenAI/Google policy page text specific to "named characters in prompts" was retrievable; findings rest on reporting of behavior and reversals.
- Rosebud's actual prompt-moderation behavior for named games is undocumented publicly.

## A3. Best feature design for "reference a game for style" — taxonomy, style cards, blocked outputs, transparency; is anyone doing exactly this?

### Takeaway
No product was found that does exactly "name a game → visible non-infringing style card." The pieces exist separately: Epic's brand-rules + DMCA strike system, Roblox's rights-holder IP Platform (licence-or-remove), Gemini/OpenAI's character blocks that preserve style, and the Copyright Office's finding that prompts alone don't confer authorship. The recommended design is a deterministic **reference → style card** conversion with the named title used only as a private lookup key.

### Cited Findings (design inputs with sources)
- Copyright Office *Copyrightability* report (Jan 29, 2025): "prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system the authors of the output"; protection can attach where AI is an assistive tool, where human-created elements are incorporated, or where a human creatively arranges/modifies AI output — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/); [Skadden](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/copyright-office-publishes-report); [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/02/copyright-office-copyrighting-ai-generated-works-requires-sufficient-human-control-over-the-expressive-elements-prompts-are-not-enough/). Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir., Mar 18, 2025) affirmed that purely AI-authored works are uncopyrightable — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102k53m/dc-circuit-affirms-ai-cannot-be-author-under-copyright-act/)
- Epic Fortnite IP/DMCA guidelines: islands cannot include content infringing third-party copyright or trademark; appearing in Fortnite does not clear third-party IP; fair use (parody/commentary) is "tricky"; each unresolved infringement earns an account **strike**, enough strikes → suspension/termination; only rights-holders can file (not the public report flow) — [Fortnite IP & DMCA Guidelines](https://www.fortnite.com/news/intellectual-property-ip-and-dmca-guidelines-for-fortnite-island-creators); [Fortnite Developer Rules](https://legal.epicgames.com/fortnite/developer-rules). Licensed partner IP (LEGO, Star Wars, TMNT, Walking Dead) has separate brand rules that forbid mixing with other third-party IP and share payout with the rights-holder (LEGO: 15% of island payout) — [LEGO Brand Rules](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/lego-brand-and-creator-rules-in-fortnite-creative)
- Roblox IP Platform Terms (last updated Sept 23, 2026): rights-holders can **offer the creator a licence** or request removal; Roblox disclaims that running the platform gives it knowledge of infringement; DMCA process; repeat infringers terminated and Robux from infringing items can be forfeited — [Roblox IP Platform Terms](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/21857240911380-IP-Platform-Terms-of-Use); [Roblox marketplace IP docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/marketplace/intellectual-property); Roblox: "the majority of complaints we see about copies of Roblox-created items do not in fact infringe on our IP" — same page
- Genie prompt guide decomposes prompts into environment / character / world sketch; practitioners find palette, camera angle and texture cues plus 1–2 verbs most effective — [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/genie/prompt-guide/); [Macaron](https://macaron.im/blog/genie-3-capabilities-limitations). Rosebud's own Genie-planner demo makes users "Choose a Genre," then biomes, actions, objectives — [Rosebud blog](https://lab.rosebud.ai/blog/google-genie-3-the-world-model-that-turns-prompts-into-playable-worlds-and-how-to-plan-one-with-a-rosebud-ai-game)
- Tetris/Spry Fox establish that the *arbitrary* audiovisual choices (colors, shapes, object progressions, characters) are what infringes, while rules/mechanics are free — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2012/06/19/videogame-app-developer-breaks-the-rules-on-copyright-infringement/); [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/?p=5131)

### Inferences (recommended design)
- **Reference resolver (deterministic, curated):** maintain an internal catalog of ~500–2,000 well-known games keyed by title/aliases ("Stardew", "SDV"). Each entry maps to a **style card** of unprotectable attributes only:
  - genre + sub-genre; core loop (e.g., "daily cycle → tend plots → sell → upgrade"); session structure (runs, levels, open-ended); progression model (meta-unlocks, skill tree)
  - camera/perspective (top-down ¾, side-scroll, isometric), resolution/pixel density (e.g., 16×16 tiles, 320×180 canvas), animation style (frame-by-frame, squash/stretch, low fps), palette descriptor (warm earth tones / neon dark), lighting mood
  - tone (cozy, frantic, melancholic), pacing, difficulty posture (forgiving / punishing), music mood (lo-fi acoustic / synth-metal), UI feel (minimal diegetic / chunky)
  - a short list of **generic mechanics** (dash with i-frames, coyote time, dialogue choices) — never the reference's proper nouns.
- **Blocked attributes (never copied):** character names/designs, place and item names, logos/wordmarks, fonts that are brand-identifying, story/dialogue, specific level/map layouts, music, UI layouts that are distinctive, and the specific "arbitrary" visual choices the Tetris court protected (e.g., exact tile colours/shapes of a known game). Also block *title/metadata* use of the trademark ("Stardew clone") on public pages; allow the name only in the private prompt log.
- **Transparency:** show the extracted card to the user ("Here's what I took from your reference: top-down farming-sim loop, 16-bit pixel art, cozy tone…") with an "edit" affordance. This (a) documents the human creative control that the Copyright Office says is needed for the user to claim authorship, (b) creates an audit trail that the platform did not instruct the model to reproduce expression, (c) improves generation quality by converting a fuzzy reference into explicit parameters.
- **Unknown titles:** when a name isn't in the catalog, run an LLM extraction into the same schema but *with the blocked-attribute filter applied* and surface the card for confirmation; never pass the raw franchise name to the asset generators.
- **Asset-generation guardrails:** strip named-IP tokens from image/music prompts; run output screening (CLIP/embedding similarity against a reference set of famous characters/logos, plus text OCR for wordmarks) before publishing; downgrade or re-roll on hits. Mirror Epic's strike system and Roblox's "licence-or-remove" option for rights-holders.
- **Licensed "official" style packs** (the Epic/LEGO model) are the only safe way to let users literally say "make a Mario level"; absent a license, treat it as a style card.

### Gaps
- No product found that implements the exact "named-game → visible style card" pattern; the recommendation is a synthesis, not a documented precedent.
- No public research found on embedding-based "looks like a famous character" screening accuracy for pixel-art outputs.

## A4. Storefront and platform policies: Steam AI disclosure (2024–2026), itch.io, Apple/Google UGC rules, and what a platform must do to let users export to Steam

### Takeaway
Steam requires a per-game generative-AI disclosure split into *pre-generated* vs *live-generated* content, narrowed in Jan 2026 to player-facing material (dev-side code assistants exempt per reporting), with live-generated content requiring described guardrails and a player report button. itch.io has required an AI disclosure since Nov 2024 (mandatory for assets; auto-tags "AI Generated"/"No AI"; untagged AI assets are de-indexed). Google Play requires in-app report/block and moderation for UGC and reporting for AI-generated content; Apple 4.7 governs HTML5 mini-games inside an app and makes the host developer responsible. An export-to-Steam feature needs the *user* to be the Steamworks publisher, with the platform supplying correct disclosure text and asset provenance.

### Cited Findings
**Steam**
- Steamworks Content Survey (official): *pre-generated* = "content that ships with your game and is consumed by players that is created with the help of AI tools during development," reviewed like non-AI content and checked against marketing promises; *live-generated* = "content created with the help of AI tools while the game is running," with the additional requirement to describe guardrails preventing illegal content; failure "will result in your app being pulled from the store"; players can report problematic live output via the Steam Overlay; Valve does not currently want live-generated adult-only sexual content; disclosures appear on store pages — [Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey); [Steamworks "AI Content on Steam" announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/eventcomments/4137185692902102798)
- January 2026 rewrite (reported Jan 16–17, 2026): disclosure scope narrowed to player-facing/shipped content (art, audio, dialogue, localization, store-page/marketing material); developer tooling such as code assistants reported exempt; generated key art or store description still triggers disclosure; >7,300 games with disclosed AI content by March 2026; Epic Games Store has no equivalent and Tim Sweeney has criticized Steam's rule — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html); [Legal Moves](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/steam-ai-policy/); [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/); [Stray Spark guide](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/steam-ai-disclosure-rules-2026-indie-developer-guide). (Valve's own changelog text not retrievable here; secondary sources disagree on exact date.)
- Valve has not published how it audits disclosures — [Legal Moves](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/steam-ai-policy/)

**itch.io**
- Nov 2024: "Generative AI Disclosure" field on project pages; "yes" asks which kinds (Graphics, Sound, Text & Dialog, Code) and auto-applies the "AI Generated" tag; "no" applies "No AI"; mandatory for asset creators, requested of all creators; untagged AI assets "will no longer be eligible for indexing on our browse pages"; grace period then reliance on user reports; rationale includes "legal ambiguity around rights associated with Generative AI content" — [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/11/itchio-store-now-requires-ai-generated-content-disclosures-for-assets); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/asset-creators-on-itch-io-now-have-to-disclose-the-use-of-generative-ai/); [Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/itch-io-mandates-genai-disclosure-for-creators/). No 2025–2026 changes found.
- itch.io does not publish ranking formulas; moderators say criteria are secret to prevent gaming; "New & Popular" adds recency weighting, "Top rated" is a confidence-weighted average (community understanding) — [itch.io forum](https://itch.io/post/10563164); [itch.io thread](https://itch.io/t/2867167/how-is-the-list-of-most-popular-engines-sorted-difference-with-most-used)

**Google Play**
- UGC policy: users must accept terms before posting; in-app report of content and users; block for 1:1 interaction features; ongoing moderation proportionate to content type; apps that "continually fail to address user complaints" violate policy; apps generating AI content must have in-app flagging; UGC apps hosting AI content fall under UGC policy; last clarified Oct 2023 — [Google Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en); [AI-Generated Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936?hl=en); [moderation requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12923286?hl=en)

**Apple**
- App Review Guideline 4.7 (revised April 2024) covers HTML5 mini apps/mini games, streaming games, chatbots, plug-ins; developers are responsible for hosted software and noncompliant content can get the host app rejected; older text required hosted code to run in standard WebKit, be free or IAP, and an index of hosted software on request — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/apple-amends-app-review-guidelines-to-permit-game-emulators-and-make-other-changes/); [Apple dev forum thread on 4.7](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/117933). Current 2025–2026 4.7 text not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Export-to-Steam checklist** for the platform: (1) the user publishes under their own Steamworks account (Steam Direct), so the platform's job is to emit an accurate, pre-filled disclosure: list which asset classes were AI-generated (art/audio/text/code), whether any runtime generation exists, and the guardrails if so; (2) ship provenance metadata (which assets are AI vs. user-uploaded vs. platform library) inside the export so the user can answer honestly; (3) if the exported build calls back to the platform's LLM at runtime (live NPC dialogue), that is "live-generated" content requiring guardrail description and a report path; (4) run the IP screen (A3) at export time, since Steam's ordinary rules already forbid infringing content and the trademark exposure is highest on a store page; (5) reflect the Jan 2026 narrowing: code written by the AI builder may not need disclosure, but every sprite/sound/line of dialogue it generated does — still safest to disclose.
- itch.io export: set the "AI Generated" disclosure automatically; expect de-indexing if omitted.
- Mobile wrappers: a platform app that plays community games is a UGC+AI app under Google Play (report/block/moderation mandatory) and an HTML5 mini-game host under Apple 4.7 (host is liable for every game).

## A5. Who owns the generated game? ToS patterns (Rosebud, Roblox, Epic) and recommendations for a remix-first community platform

### Takeaway
Patterns diverge sharply: Roblox lets creators retain copyright but takes a perpetual, irrevocable, sublicensable license and pays out via DevEx at $0.0038/Robux; Epic pays 40% of net Item-Shop revenue into an engagement pool and claims no ownership of islands but requires a license broad enough for distribution; Rosebud's ToS (per search index) asserts *Rosebud* owns prompt-generated material while marketing says "you own what you make," and takes a perpetual training license. The Copyright Office position means purely prompted output may have no copyright owner at all, which makes contractual licensing (not ownership) the practical mechanism for a remix platform.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox Terms of Use: "Creator retains all copyrights that Creator may hold in any UGC" (newer wording: creators keep "all ownership, right, title, and interest in and to your Content ... subject only to the non-exclusive license"); license to Roblox is "worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free ... perpetual, irrevocable, fully sublicensable and transferable"; "No Creator Payment or other payment is due for the grant of the license itself"; UGC must not include third-party IP without permission; Roblox may delete UGC at any time for violations — [Roblox Terms of Use](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004647846-Roblox-Terms-of-Use); [Roblox ToU update notice](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/14663412502676-Roblox-is-Updating-our-Terms-of-Use); [DevForum IP post](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/protecting-creativity-by-understanding-intellectual-property/2559638)
- Roblox DevEx: standard rate $0.0038 per Earned Robux (raised from $0.0035 in Sept 2025; 8.5% increase), higher $0.0054 tier for certain verified-US-adult purchases; only Earned Robux (after 30% marketplace fee) qualify; requires tax form and full compliance with ToU/Community Standards — [Roblox DevEx docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/developer-exchange); [Levvvel calculator (secondary)](https://levvvel.com/gaming-tools/devex-calculator/). Third-party calculators conflict on rates; the official page is authoritative.
- Epic Creator Economy 2.0 (effective March 1, 2023): 40% of net revenue from Fortnite Item Shop and related real-money purchases placed into an engagement pool shared monthly among independent creators and Epic's own islands; payouts weighted toward new/returning players for six months and only players with purchase history count — [Fortnite: Introducing the Creator Economy 2.0](https://www.fortnite.com/news/introducing-the-creator-economy-2-0?lang=en-US); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/134711/fortnite-creator-economy-40-percent-revenue). Earnings are highly concentrated in a few islands — [UEFN Central](https://uefncentral.com/can-you-make-money-on-uefn). Epic's Developer Rules require creators to hold rights or a license broad enough for Epic to distribute; creators may use Epic-provided UEFN assets even in sold items but may not recreate Epic assets not offered in UEFN; Chapter 1 map remakes are allowed but not monetizable — [Fortnite Developer Rules](https://legal.epicgames.com/fortnite/developer-rules); [Epic Chapter 1 islands post](https://www.fortnite.com/news/updating-terms-to-enable-publishing-versions-of-battle-royale-chapter-1-islands)
- Rosebud ToS (search-index excerpt; page dated ~15 months before Oct 2026): Rosebud "owns any data, content, code, artwork, or other materials created from a prompt"; free users barred from commercial use; paid users may "sell, distribute, and license what they create"; users grant a "perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable, and transferable license" to content and feedback "for any lawful business purpose, including training AI models," surviving termination; privacy policy says no opt-out for anonymized data use — [Rosebud ToS](https://rosebud.ai/terms-of-service); [Rosebud Privacy Policy](https://rosebud.ai/privacy-policy). Rosebud FAQ/marketing: paid tiers include commercial rights, "no commission on creators' earnings" — [Rosebud pricing FAQ](https://rosebud.ai/blog/pricing-subscription-faqs). Rosebud's product has a "Remix this game" button that copies any published project into the remixer's projects — [Rosebud beginner guide](https://lab.rosebud.ai/blog/beginner-guide)

### Inferences
- For a remix-first community platform, a workable pattern: (1) user retains whatever copyright exists in their human contributions (design doc, hand-edited assets, level arrangements); (2) the platform receives a non-exclusive, sublicensable license sufficient to host, display, and let others remix; (3) every published game is published under a **platform remix license** (e.g., CC-BY-style attribution chain) as a condition of publishing, since pure-AI output may be unowned and remixers need certainty; (4) remix lineage is recorded and surfaced (attribution + optional revenue split, as Epic does with LEGO 15%); (5) a clear statement on training use with an opt-out — Rosebud's silent perpetual training license and its ownership/marketing contradiction are the cautionary example; (6) no commission is a marketing lever (Rosebud) but engagement-pool payouts (Epic) scale better for a community of many small games.
- Keep the platform's own asset library under a license that permits commercial use in exported games (Epic lets creators sell items built with UEFN assets) so Steam exports are clean.

## B1. Automated metrics for 2D game quality (playability, difficulty, latency, juice, frame pacing, solvability) and the research/tools that implement them

### Takeaway
There is a mature research base for **solvability** and **difficulty** via planning/RL agents (A*-agent completion rate as in MarioGPT; A* search-effort as difficulty), a 2025 wave of **LLM-agent playtesters** (TITAN, Lap, coverage-aware LLM-guided RL, GBQA, RuleSmith), early evidence that LLMs can estimate **difficulty** and align with human **"funness"** judgments on simple games (ICLR 2026), and Claypool's precision/deadline framework for **latency** sensitivity. There are no published standard metrics for "juice" (hitstop, screen shake); those must be engineered as instrumented checks. LLM-as-judge is usable but has documented style/recency/self-preference biases.

### Cited Findings
**Solvability / playability / difficulty**
- Survey (2024): evaluating "whether or not a level can be completed, or the amount of it which can be completed" is the common playability feature in PCG evaluation — [On the Evaluation of Procedural Level Generation Systems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18657)
- MarioGPT tested 250 generated levels with Robin Baumgarten's A* agent: 88.4% completable vs ~31% for the best baseline — [MarioGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05981)
- Sokoban study: a tree-search agent with a step budget decides solvability; unsolved-within-budget = unplayable — [Level Generation Through LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05817)
- Beukman et al.: simulation-based metrics using a planning agent; difficulty = how much A* exploration/expansion is needed to solve the level; correlates with existing difficulty estimates in one domain, struggles in another — [Towards Objective Metrics for Procedurally Generated Levels](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10334)
- Agents with different personas (same metrics) generate/evaluate levels for particular player types — [Generating Game Levels of Diverse Behaviour Engagement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02100)
- Agent-free Celeste level generation evaluated playability with humans where an agent is infeasible — [U Twente, FDG 2025](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/towards-a-celeste-ai-framework-agent-free-automated-2d-level-gene/)
- "LLMs May Not Be Human-Level Players, But They Can Be Testers: Measuring Game Difficulty with LLM Agents" (ACM HCI journal, 2025) tests Wordle and Slay the Spire — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396222718_LLMs_May_Not_Be_Human-Level_Players_But_They_Can_Be_Testers_Measuring_Game_Difficulty_with_LLM_Agents)

**LLM-agent playtesting (2025–2026)**
- TITAN (arXiv 2509.22170, Sept 2025): LLM-agent framework for MMORPG testing; 95% task completion; found four previously unknown bugs in two commercial games — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22170)
- Lap (arXiv 2507.09490, July 2025): match-3 playtesting via screenshot → numeric board → LLM move → Android input; critical review notes single-run, single-game evaluation with no statistical tests — [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2507.09490)
- Coverage-aware playtesting with LLM-guided RL (arXiv 2512.12706, Dec 2025) combines code coverage with gameplay intent — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12706)
- GBQA (arXiv 2604.02648): benchmark for LLMs as QA engineers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02648); RuleSmith (arXiv 2602.06232): LLM agents as players + Bayesian optimization to balance asymmetric rules — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06232)

**LLM judgments of fun**
- "Evaluating Language Models' Evaluations of Games" (ICLR 2026): 121 novel board games, >450 human judgments on fun/fairness; compares LMs/reasoning models with humans, game-theoretic optima and symbolic agents; human split-half R²=0.82 is the alignment ceiling — [paper note](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/llm_evaluation/evaluating_language_models_evaluations_of_games/)
- LLM-as-judge biases: style bias dominant (0.76–0.92 across five judges; prefers markdown over identical plain prose) vs position bias ≤0.04 — [Judging the Judges, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23178v1); strong recency bias and provenance hierarchy — [LLMs Judge Themselves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15746); scoring bias — [Evaluating Scoring Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22316); self-preference and shortcut bias — [The Silent Judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26072v2); design choices (CoT, decoding) affect reliability — [Empirical Study of LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13639v1)

**Latency / responsiveness**
- Claypool & Claypool, "Latency Can Kill: Precision and Deadline in Online Games": classifies player actions by precision and deadline; high-precision/tight-deadline actions (FPS sniping) are sensitive to modest latency; low-precision/loose-deadline actions (RTS selection) are nearly impervious — [paper PDF](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~almeroth/classes/W10.290F/papers/claypool-10.pdf). Follow-ups: deadline matters more than precision (LoL study); QoE and scores fall linearly with added latency in cloud gaming (WPI student projects, not peer-reviewed) — [WPI](https://digital.wpi.edu/downloads/05741s25d?locale=zh)
- Swink's *Game Feel* has metric chapters for input, response and polish; Wikipedia: "no industry standard for measuring game feel" — [Game Developer excerpt](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-feel-the-secret-ingredient); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel). Input-buffer windows around 100 ms are a common practitioner illustration, not a research threshold — [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/art-of-input-buffering)

**Browser tooling**
- Playwright-based "game-qa" skill for browser games requires the game to expose `window.__GAME__`, `window.__GAME_STATE__`, `window.render_game_to_text()`, `window.advanceTime(ms)` for deterministic state inspection, visual regression, gameplay verification, performance and accessibility — [OpusGameLabs game-qa](https://skills.cat/skills/opusgamelabs/game-creator/game-qa)
- Playwright runner capturing screenshots + console errors and feeding an LLM judge back to the agent — [Bernstein docs](https://bernstein.readthedocs.io/en/latest/sandbox/playwright-self-test/); multimodal screenshot inspector — [Smithery](https://smithery.ai/skills/erichowens/playwright-screenshot-inspector)

### Inferences (implementable gate design for generated 2D web games)
- **Require a test harness contract in every generated game** (the game-qa pattern): deterministic seed, `advanceTime(ms)`, a text/JSON state dump, an input-injection API, and semantic event hooks (`onHit`, `onDeath`, `onLevelComplete`, `onPickup`). This single requirement unlocks every metric below cheaply.
- **Playability gate:** headless Playwright runs (a) a scripted/random-walk bot for crash/console-error/NaN detection, (b) a search/RL or LLM agent (TITAN/Lap style) attempting completion; fail if no agent completes within budget (Sokoban rule) or if unreachable states/dead-ends are detected via reachability over the state graph. For tile-based platformers, A*-agent completion (MarioGPT) is the proven baseline.
- **Difficulty curve:** record search effort or agent death count per level/segment (Beukman); flag non-monotonic spikes and >N consecutive deaths at one point; require a first-30-seconds "can't fail" segment. Use persona agents (cautious/aggressive) to estimate spread.
- **Responsiveness:** measure input→state-change latency inside the harness (timestamp key event → first frame where velocity changes) and input→visible feedback (first frame with a new particle/sound/animation event); Claypool's framework says precision/deadline-heavy genres (platformer, shmup) need the tightest budgets. Also check for input buffering/coyote time presence in platformers (see B2).
- **Frame pacing:** sample `requestAnimationFrame` deltas over a scripted 60-s run; gate on p95 frame time and long-task count; detect allocation churn (GC pauses) via Performance API.
- **Juice presence (instrumented, not perceptual):** on each `onHit` event assert at least K of {particle spawn, screen-shake camera offset > 0, hitstop time-scale dip, hit-flash shader/tint, SFX trigger, knockback}; on `onDeath` assert a feedback sequence; count idle/landing/attack animation states; detect "silent" games (no audio events in first 10 s). These are machine-checkable proxies of the Vlambeer/Celeste checklists in B2.
- **LLM-vision critique:** run a multimodal judge over a 10–20-frame contact sheet + a 15-s clip with a fixed rubric (readability, contrast, consistency of art style, UI legibility, feedback on actions) using pairwise comparison against a reference set rather than absolute scores, randomised order (position bias), identical formatting (style bias), and a different model family than the generator (self-preference). Use it as a *ranker/triage*, not a hard gate, given the bias literature.
- **Fun proxies:** ICLR 2026 evidence suggests LLMs align moderately with humans on "is this worth playing" for simple rule-sets; combine with behavioural proxies from agents (variance of agent outcomes, decision density, number of distinct strategies found by RuleSmith-style multi-agent play).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed metric or threshold found for hitstop/screen-shake/juice perception; "juice presence" must be defined by the platform.
- No study found validating LLM-vision critique of 2D game screenshots/videos against human quality ratings.
- Full texts of TITAN, Lap, and the difficulty-testing paper were not fetched (arxiv blocked in sandbox); findings rest on abstracts/reviews.
- No browser-native equivalent of Unity ML-Agents with a maintained ecosystem was identified; the practical path is Playwright + game-exposed hooks.

## B2. Studio polish checklists (Vlambeer screenshake, Celeste forgiveness, Hades process) → machine-checkable gates

### Takeaway
Vlambeer's "The Art of Screenshake" (JW Nijman) and Maddy Thorson's Celeste thread give concrete, enumerable techniques (coyote time, jump buffering, half-gravity apex, hit pause, muzzle flash, camera lerp, knockback, recoil, permanence) that map cleanly to presence/parameter checks. ### Cited Findings
- Celeste (Maddy Thorson): coyote time — "You can still jump for a short time after leaving a ledge"; jump buffering — "If you press and hold the jump button a short time before landing, you will jump on the exact frame that you land"; apex control — "If you hold the jump button, the top of your jump has half gravity applied"; GameSpot counts 10 game-feel techniques in the thread; Thorson says these are standard, not invented by them — [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/celeste-dev-explains-how-they-made-their-game-feel/1100-6474775/)
- Vlambeer "The Art of Screenshake" (JW Nijman, ~30 min talk): GMTK summarises Vlambeer games shaking on fire/hit and briefly pausing on kills ("pause the game for a split second when you hit or kill an enemy"); DK Liao's step-by-step recreation enumerates muzzle flash, camera lerp, hit pause, enemy knockback, weapon recoil, random enemy explosions — [GMTK "Secrets of Game Feel and Juice" transcript](https://amara.org/v/C3BGA); [DK Liao devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects); [Infovore on Nijman/Nuclear Throne](https://infovore.org/?p=5275)
- "Juice it or lose it" (Jonasson & Purho) summarised as five techniques including emphasising player success and adding randomness — [GameDev Academy](https://gamedevacademy.org/?p=11497)
- Swink's six components: input, response, context, polish, metaphor, rules — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-feel-the-secret-ingredient)
- Supergiant/Hades: Discord upvote system but "we actually read all of it"; most-upvoted ≠ most urgent (gem-earning requests were low-upvote but added); heavy internal play, watching streamers, anonymous player data to find where people got stuck; every Early Access update had to add content; Hades holds 98% positive on Steam — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/a-journey-through-early-access-helped-make-hades-a-masterpiece/); [GeekDad interview](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/); Hades II EA began May 6, 2024 with balance/narrative feedback as goals — [RPG Site 2026 interview](https://www.rpgsite.net/interview/20348-supergiant-games-interview-with-greg-kasavin-and-darren-korb-hades-ii-early-access-feedback-boss-themes-the-ending-coffee-and-the-future)

### Inferences (gate table)
| Checklist item (source) | Machine check |
|---|---|
| Coyote time (Celeste) | Inject jump N frames after leaving ledge in harness; assert jump occurs for N ≤ ~5 frames @60fps |
| Jump buffering (Celeste) | Press jump M frames before landing; assert jump on landing frame |
| Variable jump height / apex float (Celeste) | Compare short vs held jump heights; assert different; check gravity scale near apex |
| Hit pause (Vlambeer) | On `onHit`, assert time-scale < 1 or frame freeze for 1–6 frames |
| Screen shake (Vlambeer) | On `onHit`/fire, assert camera offset magnitude > 0 and decays within ≤300 ms; also assert it *stops* (no permanent jitter) |
| Muzzle flash / hit flash | On fire/hit, assert a sprite tint/flash or light sprite lifecycle |
| Knockback / recoil | On hit, assert target velocity impulse; on fire, assert camera/weapon offset |
| Particles / permanence | On hit/death, assert particle emitter burst; count persistent debris (optional) |
| Audio feedback | Every core verb has an SFX event; no SFX overlap > N per frame |
| Camera lerp | Camera follows with smoothing (no 1:1 snap); no camera leaving level bounds |
| Emphasise success (Juice it) | Level-complete triggers a ≥500 ms celebration sequence |
| Readability | Contrast between player sprite and background ≥ threshold (image analysis) |
| Feedback-loop (Supergiant) | Telemetry hooks for death position, stuck time, quit point; auto-generate "stuck heatmap" per game |

## B3. Community-driven quality signals (play time, retention, remix count, ratings) and how Roblox / Rosebud / itch.io surface good UGC and bury bad

### Takeaway
Roblox is the only platform that publicly documents its ranking: per-user-averaged engagement, retention and monetisation signals (play-through rate, play days/user, playtime/user capped at 60 min/day, first-play bounce rate, qualified plays), with ad/curation-sourced traffic excluded from ranking. itch.io keeps its formulas secret (recency-weighted "New & Popular", confidence-weighted ratings). Rosebud surfaces "trending" games and remixing, with no documented ranking or moderation.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox Home "Recommended for You" is two-stage (candidate narrowing using engagement, retention, monetisation, friend plays, similar experiences, variety; then ranking); signals are averaged per user "so smaller experiences with highly engaged users are not disadvantaged"; engagement/monetisation/retention from users first acquired via ads, curation, friends, search or social are excluded from ranking but can accelerate consideration; impressions also depend on algorithm changes, Home traffic and seasonality — [Roblox Discovery docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery); [Roblox promotion/discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/discovery)
- Roblox priority signals: play-through rate, play days per user, playtime per user, first-play bounce rate; secondary: co-play days, qualified play sessions ("filters out accidental clicks or quick bounces"), spend; analytics windows D1, D2–D7, D8–D28; playtime capped at 60 min per user per game per day; benchmark against other games the same users play — [Roblox Discovery docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/discovery)
- Roblox requires good standing with Community Standards to earn; may delete UGC for violations; DevEx requires compliance — [Roblox DevEx](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/developer-exchange); [Roblox ToU](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004647846-Roblox-Terms-of-Use)
- itch.io: moderators say ranking criteria are secret to prevent cheating; new products appear briefly at the top; "Top rated" understood as confidence-weighted average; a creator reported a sharp unexplained ranking drop after inactivity — [itch.io forum](https://itch.io/post/10563164); [itch.io thread](https://itch.io/t/2867167/how-is-the-list-of-most-popular-engines-sorted-difference-with-most-used)
- Rosebud: homepage "Browse Games" shows trending games; any game can be remixed into the remixer's projects via the watermark button; Discord runs monthly game jams with prizes and showcase streams — [Rosebud beginner guide](https://lab.rosebud.ai/blog/beginner-guide); [Rosebud Discord listing](https://discord.com/servers/rosebud-ai-773712199729414144)
- Epic engagement payouts weight new/returning players for six months and count only players with purchase history, i.e. a retention-weighted signal — [Fortnite Creator Economy 2.0](https://www.fortnite.com/news/introducing-the-creator-economy-2-0?lang=en-US)
- Hades: 98% positive Steam rating maintained from Early Access — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/a-journey-through-early-access-helped-make-hades-a-masterpiece/)

### Inferences
- Adopt Roblox's per-user-averaged signal set (bounce rate, play-through, D1/D7 return, session length capped) rather than raw totals, and exclude traffic the platform itself injected (featured, jam, share links) from ranking so featuring doesn't self-reinforce.
- Add platform-specific signals: **remix count and remix depth** (a remixed game that is itself remixed is a strong quality/inspiration signal), **completion rate of the auto-generated tutorial segment**, and **post-play rating with a mandatory one-tag reason** (fun / confusing / broken / ugly) which doubles as training data for the LLM judge in B1.
- Combine automated gates (B1) as a *pre-listing* filter (broken/unplayable games never enter the feed) with community signals for ordering, which is the Steam/itch pattern of "disclosure + ranking" rather than hard curation.

- Roblox's six-signal ranking table contents were not captured in full.
- No documentation found of Rosebud's moderation or featuring criteria.
- itch.io's official sorting post not retrieved.

## B4. Human-in-the-loop playtesting loops with community testers earning rewards

### Takeaway
Paid tester marketplaces (PlaytestCloud: ~$5–9 per 15-minute session, paid via Tremendous within ~3 days) and incentive tooling (Forge.gg tiered rewards/points/referrals; PlaytestQuest points) exist; Steam Playtest is distribution only (no rewards); Supergiant's Discord upvote + telemetry loop is the best-documented studio example; Rosebud uses monthly jams with prizes. No UGC platform was found that runs a formal rewarded community-playtest loop.

### Cited Findings
- PlaytestCloud testers: "A 15-minute playtest with a survey is typically $5 to $9 USD"; onboarding unpaid; payouts via Tremendous, typically within 3 days; no points accumulation — [PlaytestCloud rewards](https://players.playtestcloud.com/article/118-rewards-a-summary); [PlaytestCloud payout](https://players.playtestcloud.com/article/206-how-do-i-get-my-playtest-reward); [Trustpilot reply](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/playtestcloud.com?page=5)
- Steam Playtest distributes builds to beta testers through Steam infrastructure and relies on community features/external tools for data; not a paid tester network — [PlaytestCloud blog](https://start.playtestcloud.com/blog/steam-playtest-vs-playtesting-steam-games-at-playtestcloud)
- Forge.gg: configurable tiered rewards, loyalty points, referral bonuses for playtesters — [Forge.gg](https://www.forge.gg/new-features); PlaytestQuest: points plus a separate currency for higher-priority tests — [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/markendiego/project/playtest-games--earn-rewards)
- Supergiant: Discord upvotes read in full; vocal ≠ urgent; anonymous stuck-point data; content in every update — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/a-journey-through-early-access-helped-make-hades-a-masterpiece/)
- Rosebud: monthly game jams with prizes, showcase streams — [Rosebud Discord listing](https://discord.com/servers/rosebud-ai-773712199729414144)
- Google Play requires in-app reporting/flagging for UGC and AI-generated content apps — [Google Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A workable loop: (1) automated gates (B1) decide *eligibility* for human testing; (2) a "test queue" offers unreleased games to opted-in community testers with a structured 5–10-minute task (reach checkpoint, rate 4 tags, free-text) and telemetry capture; (3) rewards in platform credits (generation credits are cheaper than cash and align incentives), scaled by tester reliability (agreement with other testers and with later public ratings, the PlaytestQuest/Forge tiered pattern); (4) upvotable feedback à la Supergiant with the caveat that staff/LLM triage should also weigh low-upvote but blocking issues; (5) feed tester labels back as ground truth to calibrate the LLM judge and the juice/difficulty thresholds; (6) jams with prizes (Rosebud) as periodic high-intensity tester recruitment.
